60-0 Privacy policy

Effective October 13, 2026

60-0 is a single-player game for iOS and Android, made by hephaestuz, an independent developer. This policy explains what each app keeps on your phone, what leaves it, and who receives it. In short: the developer collects nothing. The iOS app has no ads. The Android app shows an ad only when you choose to watch one, and Google handles it.

The first part applies to both apps. Each app then has a part of its own: The iOS app and The Android app.

Both apps

This part applies to the iOS app and the Android app.

What the apps store

Each app keeps your game on your phone: the campaign in progress, recent results, best records, Daily Campaign results and streak, achievements, the commanders you have drafted, skip counts and the last mode you played. None of it is sent to the developer.

No accounts

There are no accounts and no sign-in. Neither app asks for your name, email address, contacts, location or any other personal details.

What the developer collects

Nothing. The developer runs no servers for this game, and neither app contains analytics, tracking or crash reporting of its own.

Sharing

Share sends a short text, and usually an image of your result or of a commander card, to the app you pick in your phone’s share menu. Nothing leaves your phone unless you pick a share target, and what that app does with it is covered by its own policy.

Children

60-0 is not directed at children under 13. The developer collects no personal data from anyone, children included.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will appear in the apps and on this page with a new effective date.

Contact

Questions about this policy or the app: hephaestuz76@gmail.com

The iOS app

This part applies to 60-0 from the App Store.

Storage and backup

The game is kept in one file in the app’s own storage (Application Support). The app excludes that file from iCloud backup, so it is not copied to your Apple account or restored to a new iPhone from a backup.

Permissions

The app asks for one permission, and only if you use it: the first time you save a shared image to Photos, iOS asks once whether 60-0 may add to your photos. That permission only adds images; it does not let the app see your library. Nothing else prompts you.

Crash and usage reports

If you have chosen in your iPhone’s settings to share analytics with Apple and with app developers, App Store Connect may show the developer anonymous crash and usage data. That sharing is controlled by your settings, not by the app.

Ads

The app has no ads and contains no ads or other third-party software. Apart from what you choose to share, no third party receives data from it. It does not use the advertising identifier, does not track you and never asks for permission to track you.

Fonts

The app uses the system font. Nothing is downloaded.

Sharing

The share image is handed to the share menu in memory; the app does not save it as a file.

Deleting your data

The game stays on your phone until you delete the app, and the developer never receives a copy. To delete everything the app stores, delete the app. You can turn off the Photos permission at any time in Settings, Apps, 60-0, Photos.

The Android app

This part applies to 60-0 from Google Play.

Storage and backup

App backup is turned off, so your game is not copied to your Google account backup or moved to a new phone. Google’s ads software also keeps your ad consent choices and its own working data on the phone.

Permissions

The app shows no permission prompts. The app itself asks for no permissions. The permissions it holds come from Google’s ads software: network access, the advertising ID, Android’s ad services for ad measurement and interest topics, and the background-work permissions that software needs. None of them prompts you.

Crash and performance reports

If you have chosen to share usage and diagnostics data with Google in your device settings, Google Play may show the developer anonymous crash and performance reports. That sharing is controlled by your device settings, not by the app.

Ads (Google AdMob)

The app shows ads through Google AdMob only when you choose to watch one: a rewarded video that gives a re-spin once a skip type has run out. There are no other ads.

To show and measure ads, Google’s ads software in the app collects information from your device, such as your advertising ID, IP address (from which an approximate location can be inferred), device and app details, and your interactions with ads. Google uses it under its own privacy policy, including to show personalized ads where you have allowed them. Apart from what you choose to share, Google is the only third party that receives data from the app. As the ads provider, Google is also bound by the data protection terms that govern its ads, which give that data the same or equal protection as this policy. Google’s ads software sends this information encrypted in transit.

Where the law requires it, the app asks for your choices through Google’s consent form (the User Messaging Platform) before any ad is requested. When that applies to you, you can change your choices at any time under About, Privacy choices.

You can reset or delete your advertising ID and limit personalized ads in your device settings, usually under Privacy, Ads or Google, Ads. Google ad settings: https://myadcenter.google.com. Google’s privacy policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy. How Google uses data from apps that show its ads: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites

Fonts

The app’s display font is downloaded from Google Fonts through Google Play services, and Google may log that request; if the font cannot be downloaded, a built-in font is used.

Sharing

The share image is kept in the app’s cache on your phone.

Deleting your data

Your game stays on your phone until you clear the app’s storage or uninstall it, and the developer never receives a copy. To delete everything the app stores, uninstall it, or clear its storage in Settings, Apps, 60-0, Storage. Data Google holds for ads is managed through your device and Google ad settings above. Google keeps it for the periods it describes at https://policies.google.com/technologies/retention.
